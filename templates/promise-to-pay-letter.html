What's in it

  • Acknowledgment: the buyer confirms the amount and the invoices it covers.
  • Dates: one or more pay-by dates, amounts and payment methods.
  • A heads-up clause: the buyer will tell you in writing before a missed date.
  • What happens next: if a date is missed, you may hold orders or ship cash on delivery.

How to use it

  1. Fill in the amount and invoices, and send it to the buyer to complete the dates.
  2. Ask for a date the buyer can keep. A near date that holds beats a far date that slips.
  3. Put each date on your calendar and remind the buyer the day before.
  4. If the buyer needs more than a few payments, use a payment plan agreement instead.

Frequently asked questions

What is a promise to pay letter?

A short letter, signed by the buyer, that confirms a past-due amount and commits to specific payment dates. It turns a vague intention into dates you can hold the buyer to.

What's the difference between a promise to pay and a payment plan?

A promise to pay is usually one or a few dates for a balance the buyer can clear soon. A payment plan agreement is a fuller schedule of installments, with terms for missed payments and new orders.

What if the buyer misses the date?

Follow up the same day. Then decide whether to hold new orders, move them to cash on delivery or offer a payment plan, according to your terms.
