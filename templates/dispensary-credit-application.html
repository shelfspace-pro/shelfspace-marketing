What's in it

  • Business information: legal and trade names, state cannabis license numbers and expiration, addresses and tax ID.
  • Owners and contacts: who owns the store, who buys and who pays the invoices, with an email for invoices.
  • Credit requested: the terms and limit the store is asking for and how it plans to pay.
  • References: a bank and suppliers the store already buys from on terms.
  • Terms the applicant accepts: paying by the due date, what happens when an invoice is past due, and telling you about an ownership or license change.
  • Authorization: permission to check references and business credit, and a signature.

How to use it

  1. Put your company name in the Seller line and send the Word file to the store before its first order on terms.
  2. Check the license number against your state regulator's public license lookup.
  3. Call at least one trade reference. Ask how the store pays, not just whether it pays.
  4. Set the terms and limit in the "For the seller" box, then send your written net terms policy with the approval.
  5. For a larger limit, ask an owner to sign a personal guaranty too.

Once the account is open, the hard part is getting every invoice paid on time. ShelfSpace accounts receivable sends your invoices and follows up in your name.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a credit application for every dispensary?

For any store that buys on terms, yes. It records who you are extending credit to, under which license, and what they agreed to. Stores that pay cash on delivery don't need one.

Can I edit the template?

Yes. The Word file is fully editable. Add your logo, change the terms section to match your policy, and have your counsel review the final version.

What should I check before approving credit?

Confirm the license is active with your state regulator, call a trade reference, and look at how the store has paid you before if it is an existing customer. Start with a low limit and raise it as the store pays on time.

Is this template legal advice?

No. It is a general business form. Not legal advice — have your counsel review it before you use it.
