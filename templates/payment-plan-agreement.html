What's in it

  • The balance: the store agrees what it owes and that the amount isn't in dispute.
  • Payment schedule: a table of installment dates and amounts, plus how it will pay.
  • Missed payments: a grace period, after which you can call the rest of the balance due and end the plan.
  • New orders: a choice between normal terms while the plan is current or cash on delivery until it is paid.
  • Everything else: the original invoices still stand, and early payment is always allowed.

How to use it

  1. Agree the balance and the invoice list with the buyer before you fill in the schedule.
  2. Keep the installments small enough that the store can actually make them, and the plan short enough that you can live with it.
  3. Fill in a grace period you will enforce, then have both sides sign.
  4. Remind the store before each installment is due. A plan with no reminders becomes another late balance.

On ShelfSpace, a store can request a plan from its pay link, you approve it, and every installment is reminded and collected on schedule. See payment plans on ShelfSpace. If the store only needs a date, a promise to pay letter is lighter.

Frequently asked questions

When should I offer a payment plan?

When a store wants to pay but can't clear the whole balance at once. A plan gets money moving and keeps the account. If a store won't commit to any date, a plan won't fix that.

Should I charge interest on a payment plan?

That is your call and your counsel's. Whether interest or fees are allowed depends on your agreement and your state's law. Many brands offer plans without interest to get the balance paid.

Should I keep shipping during the plan?

The template lets you choose. Shipping on normal terms rewards a store that keeps the plan current. Cash on delivery protects you while the balance is open.
