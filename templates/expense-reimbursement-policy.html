What's in it

  • What gets reimbursed: ordinary business expenses paid personally, each with a written business purpose and the company entity it belongs to.
  • Receipts: required for all lodging and for anything at the IRS receipt threshold or above, and a photo or forwarded email counts.
  • Mileage: paid at the IRS standard business rate for each trip's date, with a trip log.
  • Deadlines: the IRS safe-harbor periods for submitting expenses and returning any excess.
  • Approvals: manager first, a second approver above an amount you set, finance last, and no one approves their own.
  • Payment and exclusions: how and when people are paid back, and what the company won't pay for.

Why it's written as an accountable plan

Under IRS Publication 463, reimbursements paid under an accountable plan aren't included in the employee's wages. The plan has three rules: the expense has a business connection, the employee adequately accounts for it within a reasonable period, and any excess reimbursement is returned. The publication's safe harbor treats an expense accounted for within 60 days, with any excess returned within 120 days, as on time.

The receipt rule in the template follows the same publication: documentary evidence for all lodging and for other expenses of $75 or more. Mileage follows the IRS standard rate for the trip's date, which matters in a year like 2026 with a mid-year change. Our 2026 mileage reimbursement calculator pays each trip at the right rate.

How to use it

  1. Fill in the company, the entities it covers and who owns the policy.
  2. Set the amount that needs a second approval and name the approvers by title, so the policy survives a staff change.
  3. Choose how people are paid back and how fast, and add anything else you don't reimburse.
  4. Have your counsel review it, then have each employee sign the acknowledgment.

A policy only works if the reports follow it. In ShelfSpace expenses, receipts come in by phone camera or email, the policy's deadlines and receipt rules are flagged on each expense, approvals run in order with no self-approval, and approved reports post to Xero or QuickBooks. 1. What we reimburse

We reimburse ordinary and necessary expenses you pay personally while doing your job for the company. Every expense needs a business purpose, written on the expense when you submit it.

Each expense names the company entity it belongs to. If you work for more than one entity, submit each expense under the entity it was for, and submit one report per entity.

2. Receipts

Submit an itemized receipt for every lodging expense and for any other expense of $75 or more. A receipt shows the amount, the date, the place and what was bought. For an expense under $75 without a receipt, write down the amount, date, place and business purpose.

A photo of the receipt or a forwarded email receipt is acceptable. Keep the original until the expense is paid.

3. Mileage

We reimburse business miles driven in your own vehicle at the IRS standard business mileage rate in effect on the date of each trip. For each trip, record the date, where you started and ended, the business purpose and the miles. Commuting between home and your regular workplace is not business mileage.

4. Deadlines

Submit each expense within 60 days after you pay it. An expense submitted later may be paid as taxable wages or declined.

If you receive more than your accounted-for expenses, or an advance you don't fully use, return the excess within 120 days after the expense was paid or incurred.

5. Approvals

Your manager approves your expenses first. Reports over the amount below need a second approval, and finance gives the final approval before payment. No one approves their own expenses. A report sent back for changes returns to the submitter and starts the approvals again once it's resubmitted.

Frequently asked questions

What is an accountable plan?

A reimbursement arrangement that meets three IRS rules: the expense has a business connection, the employee accounts for it within a reasonable period, and any excess is returned. Reimbursements under it aren't wages to the employee.

Why does the policy say 60 days and 120 days?

Those are the IRS safe-harbor periods. An expense accounted for within 60 days, with any excess returned within 120 days, is treated as handled within a reasonable period.

When does an employee need a receipt?

For every lodging expense and for any other expense of $75 or more. Under $75, a written record of the amount, date, place and business purpose is enough, though many companies ask for receipts anyway.

Does this template cover state reimbursement laws?

No. It states no state law. Some states require employers to reimburse certain employee expenses, so have your counsel review it for the states you operate in.
