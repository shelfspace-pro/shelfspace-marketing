Choosing your QuickBooks accounting method

Cash, expense at receipt, or capitalize inventory: what each means, and how to switch.

  2. 0:16One card decides it
  3. 0:48Cash, and accrual at receipt
  4. 1:15Capitalize inventory

  6. 2:04Switching
Full transcript

When wholesale product lands on your dock, when should its cost hit your books? The day it arrives, the day you pay for it, or the day it sells? Each one can be right. It depends on who closes your books, and here's how to pick.

This one card, in the QuickBooks settings under My Account, decides how a wholesale purchase shows up in your books, and the option marked Current is what your store uses today. The money is the same either way. What changes is when the cost lands.

One thing it never touches: consignment. Consignment payouts always post when you approve them, as the vendor's share of what actually sold. This card is only about product you buy outright.

Your first option is Cash. The cost records when you pay the vendor, and nothing posts when the product arrives. It's the simplest, and it fits if you reconcile mostly from your bank.

The second is Accrual, expense at receipt. The moment product lands, ShelfSpace records a bill, so your books show what you owe right away, and the cost hits that same day. Most stores that do their own books pick this one.

The third option lives under Advanced, because it's meant for books an accountant closes. Switch over, and a third choice appears: Accrual, capitalize inventory.

Here, the bill posts at receipt as inventory, not as an expense. The product sits on your balance sheet, and its cost stays off your profit and loss until it sells.

In exchange, someone has to move that cost to cost of goods sold at month-end close, usually your accountant, from your register's sales. So pick this only if a bookkeeper or accountant closes your books every month.

To turn it on, accrual has to be on first, and you pick one account: the Inventory Asset account your product is held in until it sells. It's usually named exactly that.

Then you choose capitalize inventory. Before anything changes, ShelfSpace tells you exactly what it's about to do.

New bills post to your Inventory Asset account, and your open, unpaid bills re-sync to it. Bills you've already paid are left exactly as they are.

Click Change method, and it takes effect. Only your accounting admin can make this change, and you can switch back the same way, any time.

Set it once, and your purchases land in your books the way your accountant expects.
