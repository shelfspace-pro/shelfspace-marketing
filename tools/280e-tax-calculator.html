280E Tax Calculator

This 280E tax calculator shows the federal income tax a cannabis business owes when it can't deduct operating expenses, and what the same year would look like without that rule. Enter four numbers from your books. The gap between the two tax lines is what 280E costs you.

How the 280E tax calculator works

The calculator taxes gross profit under 280E and net profit without it. That follows the rule itself: the statute denies deductions and credits to a business trafficking in Schedule I or II substances, and the IRS says you can still reduce gross receipts by properly calculated cost of goods sold.

  • Taxable income under 280E is revenue minus cost of goods sold, and it never goes below zero.
  • Federal tax under 280E is that taxable income times your rate.
  • Federal tax without 280E is revenue minus cost of goods sold minus operating expenses, never below zero, times your rate.
  • Extra tax from 280E is the first tax minus the second.
  • Tax as a share of real profit is the tax under 280E divided by revenue minus cost of goods sold minus operating expenses. If that profit is zero or negative, the calculator says "No profit", because a share of zero or negative profit can't be computed. In that case you'd still owe tax only if revenue exceeds cost of goods sold.

The rate starts at 21 percent because that's the federal corporate rate. If your business is taxed as a partnership or an S corporation, the tax falls on the owners at their own rates, so ask your CPA which number to use.

A worked example, not customer data: a store with $1,000,000 in revenue, $500,000 in cost of goods sold and $400,000 in operating expenses, at the 21 percent federal corporate rate.

The sensitive input is cost of goods sold. Every dollar that properly belongs there, and isn't, gets taxed. Every dollar that's there and shouldn't be is what an examiner removes.

Which number to enter for cost of goods sold

Enter the figure your CPA would defend under the inventory rules. The IRS position is that a business under 280E uses the section 471 inventory-costing rules as they stood when 280E was enacted, and two regulations do most of the work.

  • Dispensaries. Regulation 1.471-3(b) starts with the invoice price less trade or other discounts, plus transportation and other necessary charges.
  • Cultivators and manufacturers. Regulation 1.471-11 adds direct materials, direct labor and certain indirect production costs, and it excludes marketing, selling and general administration.

Your full-year accounting cost of goods sold may include costs that belong on the other line for tax. If your books capitalize more than the tax rules allow, use your CPA's tax figure and move the difference into operating expenses. Our 280E tax guide walks through the rule in full.

Medical and adult-use in this calculator

The calculator assumes all of your activity is under 280E, and that's no longer true for every business. A federal order effective 04/28/2026 moved marijuana under a state medical license to Schedule III, while other marijuana, including adult-use, stayed in Schedule I. A DEA judge stayed the broader rescheduling case on 09/29/2026 and, by an order dated 10/22/2026, kept it stayed only until briefing closes, with replies due 11/05/2026; no final rule has been issued. Treasury and the IRS say rescheduling generally removes 280E for activity that leaves Schedule I and II, with guidance still to come.

So use it this way. An adult-use business can read the "under 280E" line as is. A medical-only business should read that line as its old exposure, and treat the "without 280E" line as a rough picture of what the announced guidance points toward, once it's actually issued. A mixed business can run the calculator twice, once for each activity's revenue, cost of goods sold and share of expenses. Treasury expects guidance on apportioning expenses, and the method isn't published, so any split you enter is your estimate.

What this calculator leaves out

This is a planning estimate for one year of federal income tax, and a real return has more in it.

  • State income tax, cannabis excise tax, sales tax and payroll tax aren't in it. Your state may or may not follow 280E, so ask your CPA.
  • Credits, loss carryforwards, interest and penalties aren't in it. A loss year simply shows zero.
  • A single flat rate applies, so it won't reflect graduated rates for owners of a pass-through.
  • All operating expenses are treated as disallowed under 280E. An IRS memo notes that a Tax Court case allowed deductions for a separate, lawful trade or business, and whether you have one is a question for your CPA.
  • The cost of goods sold figure is taken as correct. The calculator can't test it against your invoices.
  • The transition year isn't modeled. Treasury expects a rule applying the change to the full taxable year that includes the effective date, and earlier years remain unresolved.

What to take to your CPA

Take the result as a starting question, along with the records behind it. These get you a faster and better answer.

  • Your trial balance with cost of goods sold broken out by purchases, freight, discounts and vendor credits.
  • Sales and purchases by license and location, with medical and adult-use separated.
  • Vendor invoices, manifests and payments that support the cost of goods sold line. The IRS says these transactions generate the supporting documents your books rely on.
  • Three questions: which rate applies to my entity, what's my inventory method, and how will we handle medical activity for this tax year.

If your business takes the position that 280E doesn't apply, ask about it directly. In fact sheet FS-2024-32, the IRS warns that a Form 8275 disclosure lacking a reasonable basis gives no penalty protection.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate 280E tax?

Subtract cost of goods sold from revenue, then multiply by your federal tax rate. Operating expenses don't reduce that base under 280E. To see the cost of the rule, compare it with the same rate applied to revenue minus cost of goods sold minus operating expenses.

What tax rate should I enter?

The calculator starts at the federal corporate rate. If your business is a partnership or S corporation, the income is taxed to the owners at their own rates, so ask your CPA which rate fits. You can type any rate into the field.

Why can the tax be more than my profit?

Under 280E the tax is figured on gross profit, while your real profit also has rent, payroll and marketing taken out. If those costs eat most of your gross profit, the tax on the larger number can exceed what's left. The calculator shows that as tax as a share of real profit.

Does this work for a medical dispensary?

It assumes everything is under 280E, which isn't the current picture for marijuana under a state medical license. Treasury and the IRS say rescheduling generally removes 280E for that activity, with guidance still to come. Read the without-280E line as a rough picture, and have your CPA confirm it.

Is this calculator tax advice?

No. It's a one-year federal estimate that leaves out state taxes, credits, losses, penalties and your specific inventory method. Take the result and your records to your CPA.
