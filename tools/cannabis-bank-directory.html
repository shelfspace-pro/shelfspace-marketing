How to use this cannabis bank directory

Filter the list by your state, then start with the institutions that name your state and your license type. Every entry links to the institution's own page, and we included it only because that page publicly says it serves cannabis businesses. Multi-state entries, and entries whose site doesn't state a coverage area, may also cover you, so check those too.

A listing tells you an institution advertises a cannabis program. It doesn't mean the program is taking new customers, that it accepts your license type or that it would approve you. We don't rank these institutions, none paid to appear here, and this list isn't complete, since FinCEN's data through 12/31/2024 counts far more filers than we list. Your state may also publish its own resource, as Washington's financial regulator does.

What to ask a cannabis friendly bank

Ask whether the program is open to your business right now, then ask what it will really cost and how the relationship works. These questions save you from a long application that ends in a no.

  • Are you accepting new cannabis accounts today? Programs change, and one bank's page now says it has stopped opening them.
  • Do you take my license type and state? Ask about plant-touching and ancillary businesses, pending licenses, and any locations in other states.
  • What's the full fee schedule? Ask for opening fees, monthly fees, any percentage of deposits, annual review fees and per-item charges, in writing.
  • How do I deposit cash? Ask about armored pickup, smart safes, deposit limits and when funds become available.
  • Can I pay vendors electronically? Ask about ACH origination, wires, bill pay and payroll, and any limits on them.
  • Do you lend to cannabis businesses? Some do, and others say they can't, so ask before you assume.
  • How are my deposits insured? Ask for the name of the institution holding your money and the type of insurance that covers it.
  • What will you need from me each month and each year? Evergreen Credit Union, for one, says monthly and annual compliance requirements apply for the life of the relationship.
  • How long will this take, and who's my contact? Get a named person and a realistic date.

What to have ready before you call

Have your licenses, formation documents, tax ID, owner details and expected monthly deposits in hand before your first call. Institutions work from FinCEN's guidance, which expects them to verify your license and understand your normal activity, so a complete file gets you a faster answer. Frequently asked questions

Which banks work with cannabis businesses?

Many community banks and credit unions do, along with a few multi-state programs. Our directory lists institutions whose own websites say they serve cannabis businesses. Others may serve the industry without advertising, so also ask your state's regulator or trade association.

Is there a cannabis credit union?

Many credit unions run cannabis programs, and you can filter for them in this directory. A credit union also has membership rules, so check that your business qualifies by location or another requirement before you apply.

Does being on this list mean a bank will approve me?

No. A listing only means the institution says publicly that it serves cannabis businesses. Each one decides for itself, based on your license, your state, your owners and your expected activity, and any program can stop taking new accounts.

How did you choose who's listed?

We included an institution only when its own website publicly said it serves cannabis businesses and we read that page. We don't rank anyone or list fees, and no institution paid to appear. Tell us if something has changed and we'll check it.
