Why put your terms in writing

Unwritten terms drift. A store that was never told when an invoice is due, or what happens when it's late, has nothing to hold itself to. A short written policy, sent before the first order on terms, settles those questions before money is owed.

What the policy covers

  • Payment terms: how many days a store has to pay, counted from the invoice date.
  • Credit limits: the most a store can owe you at once.
  • Opening an account: a credit application, an optional personal guaranty, and the store's current license.
  • How to pay: the payment methods you accept.
  • Reminders: when stores hear from you about an invoice.
  • Past-due accounts: when new orders go cash on delivery or wait, and that a written payment plan is an option.

What it leaves to you and your lawyer

The builder doesn't add late fees or interest. Whether you can charge them, and how much, depends on your contracts and your state. Frequently asked questions

What payment terms should a cannabis brand offer dispensaries?

Pick terms you can afford to finance and will actually enforce. Many brands start new accounts on cash on delivery for the first order, then move to terms once the store has paid on time. Whatever you choose, put it in writing before the first order on terms.

Should I require a personal guaranty?

It is common for new accounts or larger credit limits, because it gives you someone to collect from if the business can't pay. Some buyers will push back. Our free personal guaranty template is a starting point for your counsel.

Can I add late fees to this policy?

The builder leaves them out on purpose. Whether a late fee or interest is allowed depends on your signed agreement and your state's law, so ask your counsel before you add one.
