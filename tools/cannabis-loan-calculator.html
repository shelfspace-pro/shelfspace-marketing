Cannabis Loan Calculator: Monthly Payment, Total Cost and Effective APR

This calculator shows what a loan really costs once the fee and the full term are counted. Enter the loan amount, annual rate, term in months and origination fee. You'll get the monthly payment, total interest, fee, total cost and effective APR. The monthly payment is what you'll feel, and the total cost and effective APR are what you should compare.

How to read each result

Each result answers a different question, so read all five before you decide.

  • Monthly payment. The level amount due each month, assuming a standard amortizing loan. Compare it against the cash you actually collect in a slow month, not your best one.
  • Total interest. What you pay over the full term beyond the loan amount. A longer term lowers the payment and usually raises this number.
  • Fee. The origination fee in dollars. If the lender withholds it from the funds, you receive less than the loan amount and still repay the full amount.
  • Total cost. Interest plus the fee. This is the cleanest number for comparing two offers that run for the same term.
  • Effective APR. The annual rate once the fee is counted against the cash you receive. The CFPB describes APR as a measure of the interest rate plus additional fees charged with the loan, which is why a loan's APR runs higher than its stated rate when it carries a fee.

Monthly payment on a fifty-thousand-dollar business loan

The monthly payment on a $50,000 business loan depends entirely on the rate and term, and the lender's fees change what the loan really costs, so there is no single answer until you enter your numbers. The rate below only shows the math and isn't a typical rate, or a quote.

In a worked example, not customer data, a $50,000 loan at 12% over 36 months with a 2% origination fee has a monthly payment of $1,660.72, with total interest of $9,785.92 and a $1,000 fee, for a total cost of $10,785.92 and an effective APR of about 13.42%, using the fee treatment the CFPB's APR explanation describes.

In that same worked example, not customer data, stretching to 60 months at the same stated rate and fee cuts the payment to $1,112.22 and raises total cost to $17,733.20, while the effective APR falls to about 12.90% because the fee is spread over more time. That's the trap in comparing by APR alone: the longer loan shows the lower APR and costs far more in dollars.

How to compare two cannabis loan offers

Compare two offers by putting them on the same footing: the cash you receive, the time you hold it and the dollars you pay back. Lenders quote in different formats, and a quote that looks cheaper can lose once you line these up.

  • Start with the cash you receive. Enter the full loan amount and count every upfront charge as the fee. The effective APR is the rate at which all your payments equal that net cash, so a bigger fee means a higher APR on the same loan.
  • Run both at the same term if you can. Ask each lender to quote the term you actually want. If the terms differ, compare the monthly payment, the total cost and the effective APR side by side, because no single number settles it.
  • Ask how long you'll really keep the loan. A fee paid at closing costs more per year if you refinance early, since you've paid all of it for less time. If you expect to pay off early, run the calculator again at that shorter term for a rough read, and check the prepayment terms.
  • Test the slow month. Hold the payment against your weakest recent month of collections. If it only works in your best month, the loan is too big or the term is too short.
  • Count the cost of the collateral. Two loans with the same APR aren't equal if one takes a blanket lien and a personal guaranty and the other takes a lien on one piece of equipment.

What to watch in cannabis loan terms

The riskiest parts of a cannabis loan are often outside the rate, so read the whole agreement against this list before you sign. Have your attorney read it too.

  • Nobody may have to show you an APR. The federal Truth in Lending rule, 12 CFR 1026.3(a), exempts credit primarily for a business purpose, so ask for the APR in writing or calculate it here.
  • Net proceeds. Ask what lands in your account after origination, legal, diligence and broker fees. Add every charge you must pay to get the money into the fee field.
  • Prepayment. Some deals charge a penalty, or a minimum interest amount, if you pay early. Refinancing later may cost more than you expect.
  • Balloon payments. A low payment can leave a large balance due at the end. This calculator assumes the loan fully pays off, so a balloon needs its own math and a real plan for repaying it.
  • Personal guaranties and liens. Find out what the lender can reach if the business fails, including your home, your other entities and your receivables. A blanket lien can also block you from other financing later.
  • Disclosure to your regulator. Some states vet or record lenders. Washington's WAC 314-55-035 requires background checks on financiers, and California's 4 CCR 15004 makes a person providing a loan a disclosable financial interest holder, with banks excluded.
  • Covenants and reporting. Many agreements require regular financial statements and minimum ratios. Late or sloppy reporting can put you in default even when every payment is on time.
  • Control and license terms. Watch for provisions that give a lender a share of profits or a say over operations, because those can trigger a license problem on top of a business problem.

When this calculator doesn't fit your offer

This calculator models a standard fixed-rate loan with level monthly payments and a fee paid at closing. Other structures need different math, even if the seller calls them a loan.

Merchant cash advances charge a factor rate and take daily or weekly payments. New York Financial Services Law 803 requires an estimated APR on sales-based financing, based on projected payments and term, so ask for that figure wherever you are. Our cannabis business loans guide walks through how to annualize one.

Variable rates, interest-only periods and leases also behave differently. Ask for the full payment schedule, and calculate the rate from the cash you receive and every payment you make. An equipment lease may also add an end-of-term buyout that belongs in your total.

Frequently asked questions

What is the monthly payment on a $50,000 business loan?

It depends on the rate and term. In a worked example, not customer data, $50,000 at 12% has a payment of $1,660.72 over 36 months and $1,112.22 over 60 months. Enter your own offer in the calculator, since those numbers show the math and aren't a typical rate.

What is effective APR on a loan?

It's the annual rate once fees are counted against the cash you actually receive. A loan with a stated rate and an origination fee has a higher effective APR than its stated rate. Compare it alongside total dollar cost, since a longer term can lower the APR and raise the dollars you pay.

Can I use this for a merchant cash advance?

Not directly. A cash advance uses a factor rate with daily or weekly payments, and this calculator assumes level monthly payments. Ask the funder for an estimated APR, or calculate one from the net cash you receive and the real payment schedule.

Does this calculator mean I'll qualify for a loan?

No. It only does the math on the numbers you enter, and any real rate and approval come from the lender. We aren't a lender, but we can get your AR aging, payment history and reporting in shape before you apply.
