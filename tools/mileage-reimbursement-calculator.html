2026 Mileage Reimbursement Calculator

The IRS business rate is 72.5¢ a mile for trips through 06/30/2026 and 76¢ a mile from 07/01/2026. Enter each trip's date and miles, and the calculator pays every trip at the right rate.

2026 is the first year in a while with two business mileage rates. The IRS set the rate at 72.5 cents a mile starting 01/01/2026, then raised it to 76 cents a mile for trips from 07/01/2026, citing the cost of fuel. A mileage report that applies one flat rate to the whole year pays somebody the wrong amount.

The first-half rates come from the IRS release of 12/29/2025. The July change came in IRS Announcement 2026-11, as the Journal of Accountancy reported: business rose to 76 cents and medical and moving to 23.5 cents, while the charitable rate is set by statute at 14 cents and didn't move.

How to calculate mileage reimbursement

  1. Log the business miles for each trip, with the date.
  2. Find the rate for that date: 72.5 cents through 06/30/2026, 76 cents from 07/01/2026.
  3. Multiply the miles by the rate and round that trip to the cent.
  4. Add the rounded trips. That total is the reimbursement.

A worked example, not customer data: a sales rep drives 40 business miles on 06/12/2026 and 40 more on 07/14/2026, and turns both in on one report in July.

In this worked example, not customer data, paying all 80 miles at the July rate would give $60.80, which overpays the June trip by $1.40.

Round each trip before you add them up, the way the report pays them. Adding unrounded amounts and rounding the total can land a cent away from the sum of what each trip shows.

Why the trip date decides the rate

The new rates took effect for driving on and after 07/01/2026, so what counts is when the miles were driven, not when the report was filed or approved. A June trip submitted in August still gets the June rate. That's the mistake to watch for in a mid-year rate change: a report filed in July that mixes June and July driving needs both rates.

Mileage reimbursement under an accountable plan

Under an accountable plan, the reimbursement isn't taxed to the employee. IRS Publication 463 lists the three rules: the expense has a business connection, the employee adequately accounts for it within a reasonable period, and any excess reimbursement is returned. When all three are met, the employer leaves the reimbursement out of box 1 of the employee's W-2.

The same publication gives safe-harbor timing: an employee who accounts for an expense within 60 days after it was paid or incurred, and returns any excess within 120 days, meets the reasonable-period rule. Reimbursement that misses the rules is generally treated as paid under a nonaccountable plan, which means wages.

For mileage, accounting for the trip means a log with the date, the destination, the business purpose and the miles. A monthly total with no trips behind it is the record an auditor pushes back on.

What each trip in the log should show

  • Date, which also sets the rate in 2026.
  • Start and end, by address or by place, like the office to a store.
  • Business purpose, such as a delivery, a store visit or a bank run.
  • Business miles, with any personal stop taken out.

Frequently asked questions

What is the IRS mileage reimbursement rate for 2026?

72.5 cents a mile for business trips from 01/01/2026 through 06/30/2026, and 76 cents a mile for business trips from 07/01/2026 through 12/31/2026. The IRS raised the rate mid-year, citing higher fuel costs.

Which rate applies to a June trip I submit in July?

The rate for the date you drove. A trip on 06/30/2026 is paid at 72.5 cents a mile even if the expense report goes in on 07/15/2026.

How do I calculate mileage reimbursement?

Multiply the business miles for each trip by the rate in effect on that trip's date, round each trip to the cent, then add the trips up. The calculator above does exactly that.

Is mileage reimbursement taxable to the employee?

Not when it's paid under an accountable plan: the trip has a business purpose, the employee accounts for it on time, and any excess is returned. Reimbursement under a plan that doesn't meet those rules is treated as wages. Ask your CPA how your plan is set up.

Does an employer have to reimburse mileage at the IRS rate?

The IRS rate is the standard rate, not a federal requirement to pay it. Many employers use it because it's simple and documented. Some states have their own reimbursement rules, so check with your counsel.
