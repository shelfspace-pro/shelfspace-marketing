43% of a Brand's Past-Due Invoices Paid in the First Month

A cannabis brand handed us its past-due stores. Within 30 days, 43% of what was past due had been paid. We guarantee 25%.

At a glance

  • A wholesale brand on net-30 terms with dozens of past-due invoices
  • We connected QuickBooks and took over the follow-up, in the brand's own name
  • 43% of the past-due balance was paid within 30 days
  • In a typical month before, about 30% came in
  • Invoices more than 60 days late: 33% paid, up from about 20%

The Setup

A wholesale brand sold to stores on net-30 terms. On the day it started with us, a few dozen invoices were past due, and most of that balance was more than 60 days late.

Nobody had stopped caring. The sales team was busy selling, and chasing a buyer you're about to pitch is the job everyone puts off. So the oldest balances sat.

What We Did

The brand connected QuickBooks. We loaded every open invoice, sent each past-due store its invoice with a Pay Now link, and followed up on a steady schedule, every message in the brand's own name.

How We Measured It

We took every invoice that was past due on day one and checked QuickBooks for payments against it over the next 30 days. Then we ran the same test on each of the five months before, so the result has something to stand next to.

  • Before: in a typical month, about 30% of the past-due balance came in, and about 20% of anything more than 60 days late.
  • First month with us: 43% of the past-due balance came in, and 33% of anything more than 60 days late.

The second line is the one that matters. Money that's two months late has usually stopped moving. In the first month, it came in at about 1.6 times the brand's usual rate.

How It Works

1

Send the invoice with a Pay Now link

One link for the balance. No login, and the store pays from its own bank.

2

Follow up on a steady schedule

Reminders go out on a set cadence, in the brand's own name, so no balance sits unasked.

3

Stop the moment it's paid

When the payment lands, the reminders stop on their own and QuickBooks is updated.

Stores that can't pay in full can be offered interest-free installments, and each sales rep can get a Monday list of who owes what on their accounts. That's all part of ShelfCollect.

The Result

43% of the past-due balance was paid in the first month, well past the 25% we guarantee. The biggest change was on the oldest money, the balances the brand had stopped expecting.

Late money doesn't come back on its own. It comes back when someone asks, every time, on schedule.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much past-due cannabis AR can a brand expect to collect in the first month?

It depends on how old the balances are and who owes them. We guarantee 25% of your past-due invoices in the first month, or you don't pay. This brand saw 43%.

Do the stores know a collections service is involved?

No. Every invoice and reminder goes out in the brand's own name, and replies can go straight to the brand's own inbox. The store hears from its vendor, not a collection agency.
