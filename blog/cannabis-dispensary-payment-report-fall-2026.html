How Fast Dispensaries Pay Cannabis Brands: Fall 2026 Data

A first look from our own books: how long dispensaries take to pay the cannabis brands that supply them, how much is past due, and what happens once someone keeps asking. Updated every quarter.

At a glance

  • On 10/27/2026, 70% of open wholesale invoices on the books of four cannabis brands were past due, covering 73% of the open dollars.
  • 152 of 191 dispensaries (80%) with an open invoice had at least one that was past due.
  • Invoices paid this fall took a median of 33 days from invoice date to payment. Median terms were net 30.
  • 20% of payments arrived more than 30 days after the due date, and 8% more than 60 days after.
  • Of invoices already past due when brands started with ShelfSpace, 40% of the dollars were paid within 30 days.

Every cannabis brand that sells on terms asks the same question: how long do dispensaries take to pay, and is my book normal? The industry figures most people quote are a few years old. So we pulled our own. This report covers wholesale invoices from four cannabis brands, three in Massachusetts and one in Michigan, to the dispensaries they sell to. It is a first look, and we will update it every quarter as more brands join.

On 10/27/2026, the four brands had 438 open invoices out to 191 dispensaries. Most of them were already late.

How long dispensaries take to pay

Between 09/14/2026 and 10/26/2026, the brands recorded payment on 445 invoices from 232 dispensaries. Median terms on those invoices were net 30.

Measured against the due date instead of the invoice date, 20% of payments arrived more than 30 days late, and 8% arrived more than 60 days late. A net-30 invoice that gets paid on day 33 is close to on time. A meaningful slice of invoices drift well past that, and those are the ones that strain a brand's cash.

What happens once someone keeps asking

When each brand started with ShelfSpace, 207 of its invoices to 136 dispensaries were already past due. The median one was 17 days late, and 19% were more than 90 days late. From that day on, every invoice went out with a one-click pay link and a steady schedule of reminders in the brand's own name.

That matches what we hear from brands every week. Most dispensaries that pay late are not refusing to pay. They are short on cash that week, waiting on an answer about the invoice, or nobody asked. Make paying take one click and keep asking on a schedule, and a large share of the money comes in.

How this compares to industry figures

Outside estimates point the same way, though most are older. The Cannabiz Credit Association has reported that more than 56% of cannabis invoices nationwide are past due. Whitney Economics estimated $3.8 billion in delinquent payments across the industry, based on 2023 data. LeafLink reported that delinquent payments in the U.S. cannabis industry surpassed $4 billion in 2024. Our 70% figure is higher than the 56% national estimate, which fits a sample of brands that started with a past-due problem.

Methodology

Sample. Wholesale invoices from four cannabis brands (three in Massachusetts, one in Michigan) to the dispensaries they sell to, as managed in ShelfSpace and synced from each brand's QuickBooks Online. Four brands in two states is a small sample, and it is not a random one: brands often start with ShelfSpace because they have past-due invoices.

Open-book snapshot. All open invoices with a due date on 10/27/2026. An invoice is past due when its due date is before 10/27/2026.

Days to pay. Invoices with a payment recorded between 09/14/2026 and 10/26/2026. Days to pay runs from the invoice date to the date the payment was recorded in the brand's QuickBooks, which can trail the day the store actually paid by a few days.

Past-due backlog. Invoices already past due on the day each brand started with ShelfSpace, followed from that day forward.

Privacy. Demo and test data are excluded. Every figure is aggregate and de-identified. We publish no brand names, store names, dollar totals or single-brand breakouts.

Frequently asked questions

How long do dispensaries take to pay cannabis brands?

In ShelfSpace's Fall 2026 data from four cannabis brands in Massachusetts and Michigan, invoices paid between 09/14/2026 and 10/26/2026 took a median of 33 days from invoice date to payment. The middle half took 12 to 52 days, and 1 in 10 took 74 days or more. Median terms were net 30.

What share of cannabis wholesale invoices are past due?

On 10/27/2026, 70% of the open wholesale invoices on the books of four cannabis brands using ShelfSpace were past due, covering 73% of the open dollars. Brands often start with ShelfSpace because they already have past-due invoices, so this snapshot leans toward slower-paying accounts. The Cannabiz Credit Association has separately reported that more than 56% of cannabis invoices nationwide are past due.

How late are past-due cannabis invoices?

In the 10/27/2026 snapshot, the median past-due invoice was 23 days late. Of past-due invoices, 62% were 1 to 30 days late, 23% were 31 to 60 days late, 5% were 61 to 90 days late, and 11% were more than 90 days late.

Do past-due cannabis invoices get paid once someone follows up?

Most of the money does start moving. Across four brands, 207 invoices to 136 dispensaries were already past due on the day each brand started with ShelfSpace. 40% of those past-due dollars were paid within 30 days of starting, and 53% have been paid to date.

How was this cannabis payment data collected?

The figures come from wholesale invoices that four cannabis brands manage in ShelfSpace, synced from their QuickBooks Online. Payment date is the date the payment was recorded in the brand's QuickBooks. Demo and test data are excluded, and every figure is aggregate and de-identified: no brand, store or dollar total is published.
