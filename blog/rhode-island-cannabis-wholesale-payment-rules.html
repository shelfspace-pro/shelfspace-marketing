Rhode Island Cannabis Delinquent Payments: The 30-Day Rule Every Purchase Order Needs

Rhode Island requires a written agreement for every cannabis sale between licensees, with a payment due date. Leave the date blank and the rule fills it in: 30 calendar days from delivery.

At a glance

  • Checked 10/27/2026. The rule is 560-RICR-10-10-2, section 2.11, from Rhode Island's Cannabis Control Commission (amended effective 03/17/2026).
  • A retailer may only buy cannabis from a Rhode Island licensed cultivator or manufacturer it has a formal agreement with.
  • Every sale needs a written, executed contract or purchase order with a price, a delivery window of no more than 30 calendar days, and a payment due date.
  • If the parties don't agree on a payment date, payment is due within 30 calendar days of delivery or pickup.
  • Purchase orders can't be modified. They can be cancelled or voided by a new replacement order.

Rhode Island doesn't leave payment timing to a handshake. Every cannabis sale between licensees runs on a written agreement, and the agreement has to say when the money is due. If it doesn't, the rule sets the date for you.

For a cultivator or manufacturer selling into Rhode Island retailers, that's a useful rule. It turns "they'll pay when they pay" into a date you can point to.

What the rule says

Section 2.11 of the Cannabis Control Commission's operational rules says a compassion center, hybrid cannabis retailer, or other retail cannabis establishment may only buy cannabis or cannabis products from a Rhode Island licensed cultivator or manufacturer it has a formal agreement with. The rule then spells out what that agreement must contain:

560-RICR-10-10-2 § 2.11(D)(1)

“A written executed contract or purchase order shall be required for all sales or services and shall contain the following minimum terms: … The specific date or date range not spanning more than thirty (30) calendar days for fulfillment of the order, performance of the services, and delivery or pickup; The payment due date, as specifically agreed between the parties, but if no date is specifically agreed to, payment shall be made within thirty (30) calendar days from the date of delivery or pickup”

The same list requires the date the order was placed, a description and amount of product, and the total and per-unit price. Contracts and purchase orders can't be modified, only cancelled or voided by a new replacement order.

The rule isn't new in spirit. Rhode Island's earlier medical-program rules had the same 30-day default, and in 2023 news outlets reported that state regulators fined a Providence compassion center $10,000 over late payments to cultivators. We cite that as reported news, not as the current rule.

How to use it when a store is late

A weak position

No written order, or an order with the due date left vague. You're arguing about what was agreed instead of asking for money that's late.

A strong position

An executed order with a due date (or the 30-day default), the delivery date on record, and a dated trail of every reminder and promise since.

  • Put a due date on every purchase order. If you want shorter terms than 30 days, write them in. If you leave it blank, the default is 30 calendar days from delivery or pickup.
  • Know your delivery dates. The clock runs from delivery or pickup, so that date has to be on record.
  • Don't "adjust" an old order. The rule says orders can't be modified. If terms change, issue a replacement.
  • Ask the Commission how it wants a violation raised. We didn't find a formal reporting process in the rule itself, so check with the Cannabis Control Commission and your attorney before you file anything.

Frequently asked questions

When does a Rhode Island dispensary have to pay a cannabis cultivator?

On the payment due date written in the contract or purchase order. If no date is specifically agreed, 560-RICR-10-10-2 section 2.11 says payment must be made within thirty calendar days from the date of delivery or pickup.

Does Rhode Island require a written contract for cannabis sales between licensees?

Yes. A retailer may only buy from a Rhode Island licensed cultivator or manufacturer it has a formal agreement with, and a written executed contract or purchase order is required for every sale, with minimum terms including price, delivery window and payment due date.

Can a Rhode Island purchase order be changed after it is signed?

No. The rule says contracts and purchase orders may not be modified, but they may be cancelled or voided by a new replacement contract or purchase order.

How does a brand report a late payment in Rhode Island?

We did not find a formal reporting process in the rule itself. Check with the Cannabis Control Commission and your attorney before you file anything.
