California Cannabis Delinquent Payments: Two Bills Failed, So Your Contract Is the Rule

California lawmakers tried twice to make cannabis licensees pay invoices on time. Both bills died. Here's what they would have done, and what a brand can do today when a store won't pay.

At a glance

  • Checked 10/27/2026. We found no California law or Department of Cannabis Control (DCC) rule that sets payment terms between cannabis licensees.
  • AB 766 (2023) would have required paying invoices of $5,000 or more within 15 days after the invoice's final date, with unpaid invoices reported to the DCC. It failed.
  • AB 2888 (2024) tried a similar approach and was held in committee. It also failed.
  • Both bills would have barred a buyer with an unpaid invoice from buying on credit until it paid.
  • Without a state rule, the contract and your follow-up decide when you get paid.

California has the biggest cannabis market in the country, and some of the loudest complaints about stores that don't pay. Brands backed a fix in Sacramento twice. Both times it died. So in California, as of October 2026, the answer to "is there a rule?" is no.

What the law says today

We found no California statute or DCC regulation that sets payment deadlines, credit limits or a delinquent list for sales between cannabis licensees. Your contract and invoice terms govern, and your remedies are the ordinary ones: stop shipping on credit, negotiate, or go to court.

The two bills that tried

AB 766 (2023): Cannabis: invoices: payment

As described in the bill's digest, AB 766 would have:

1

Set a deadline

Required a licensee to pay for goods and services worth $5,000 or more from another licensee within 15 days after the invoice's final payment date. The final date could be no more than 30 calendar days after the sale.

2

Required a report

A seller still unpaid 15 days after that date would report the invoice to the DCC.

3

Added consequences

If the buyer still hadn't paid 30 days after the DCC's notice, the DCC would issue a warning or, at its discretion, a citation or disciplinary action.

4

Cut off credit

A licensee with an outstanding invoice couldn't buy from another licensee on credit until it paid in full.

AB 766 was heard in the Assembly Business and Professions Committee in April 2023 and never passed. On 02/01/2024 it was filed with the Chief Clerk under Joint Rule 56, which ended it.

AB 2888 (2024): Cannabis: invoices: payment

AB 2888 took the same approach: pay within 15 days after the invoice due date, report an unpaid invoice to the DCC, a warning or discipline if it stayed unpaid 30 days after notice, and no credit purchases until the debt was paid. On 05/16/2024 it was held under submission in committee. It also failed.

What brands do instead

With no state process, brands have built their own. Some California brands and distributors share lists of slow-paying retailers through industry credit groups. Those are private arrangements, not state law. Most of the work still falls on the brand itself:

How invoices go bad

Terms agreed loosely, follow-up left to a busy sales rep, and nothing written down when a buyer says it will pay next week.

How brands get paid

A due date on every invoice, a pay link in every message, reminders on a schedule, every promise logged and checked, and a final notice before it goes to a lawyer.

  • Write the terms down. Due date and amount on every invoice.
  • Start early. Ask the day an invoice comes due, not the day it hits 90.
  • Stop shipping on credit to stores that don't pay. It's the lever you control.
  • Use a final notice before court. One dated letter with a pay-by date settles a lot of accounts that four reminders didn't.

Frequently asked questions

Does California have a law on how fast dispensaries must pay cannabis brands?

Not as of 10/27/2026. We found no California statute or Department of Cannabis Control regulation that sets payment terms between cannabis licensees. The contract and invoice terms govern.

What was California AB 766?

A 2023 bill titled Cannabis: invoices: payment. It would have required licensees to pay invoices of $5,000 or more within 15 days after the invoice's final date, required sellers to report unpaid invoices to the DCC, and barred a buyer with an unpaid invoice from buying on credit. It failed and was filed with the Chief Clerk under Joint Rule 56 on 02/01/2024.

What happened to California AB 2888?

AB 2888 (2024) proposed a similar 15-day payment deadline and DCC reporting process. It was held under submission in committee on 05/16/2024 and failed.

What can a California brand do about a dispensary that won't pay?

Put the due date on every invoice, follow up on a schedule, stop shipping on credit to stores that don't pay, send a final notice with a pay-by date, and talk to your attorney about court if the balance is large.

Is this legal advice?

No. This page explains public bills and their status in plain English. Check with your own attorney before you act on it.
