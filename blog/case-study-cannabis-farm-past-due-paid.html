At a glance

  • A small cannabis farm selling wholesale to stores
  • $22,099 past due across 10 invoices, most a month or more late
  • We sent each one with a Pay Now link and a steady reminder schedule
  • Two days after the first reminders, four invoices were paid
  • $19,531 paid within 30 days: 9 of the 10 invoices
  • Every email went out in the farm's own name

The Setup

A small farm. The people who grow it also sell it, deliver it and send the invoices. There is no AR department. There's whoever has a minute on Friday.

When it came to us, $22,099 was past due across 10 invoices, and most of it wasn't new. The typical invoice was more than a month late. For a farm this size, that's payroll.

The stores weren't refusing to pay. Nobody had asked them twice.

What We Did

We loaded the farm's open invoices, matched each one to the store that owed it, and started following up.

1

Send every open invoice with a Pay Now link

One link per balance, in the brand's own name. No login. The store pays from its own bank.

2

Follow up on a steady schedule

Reminders go out on a set cadence until each invoice is paid, so no balance sits unasked.

3

Stop the moment it's paid

When the payment lands, the reminders stop and QuickBooks is updated.

Every message went out under the farm's name. For a small grower that matters: the relationship with each store is the business. That's ShelfSpace.

What Came In

Two days after the first reminders went out, four invoices were paid. More followed over the next two weeks.

Within 30 days, 9 of the 10 past-due invoices were paid: $19,531, or 88% of the balance. The median invoice was paid 16 days in. It took 12 reminders across all of them. That's it.

Every invoice under 60 days late was paid in full. The stores had the money. They needed a clear amount, a link to pay it, and a reminder that showed up on time.

What This Doesn't Prove

Ten invoices is a small sample. A farm with a hundred stores will see a different mix, and some of these stores might have paid on their own eventually.

One invoice, more than 90 days late, is still open. Balances that old usually need more than a reminder: a final notice letter, a phone call, or a payment plan.

The Result

$19,531 back in a small farm's bank account in a month, from invoices that had been sitting for weeks. Nobody at the farm stopped growing to chase it.

Most stores aren't dodging you. They just haven't been asked clearly, on time, with a way to pay in one click.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a small cannabis farm need collections help?

A small farm usually has no one whose job is AR, so past-due invoices sit. In this case 10 invoices, most a month or more late, were 88% paid within 30 days once someone followed up on a schedule.

Will payment reminders hurt my relationship with dispensaries?

They go out in your name, politely, on a steady schedule, and stop the moment an invoice is paid. Stores see a vendor running a tight ship, not a collection agency.
