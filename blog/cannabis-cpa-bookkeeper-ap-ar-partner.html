What we actually do, in three steps

The whole engagement is easy to explain to a client, because it's three moves — verify, pay, reconcile — and the operator stays in control of the one that matters.

1

Verify what's owed against Metrc.

Every vendor invoice gets three-way matched against the Metrc manifest and the physical delivery. Duplicate lines, phantom charges, and short shipments surface before a dollar moves — so the client pays what they actually received, not what the invoice claims.

2

Move payments on the operator's approval.

The operator approves; we cut the payment. Vendors get a Check 21 check they can deposit — the rail that keeps working when card networks won't touch cannabis. Nothing leaves the account without a human sign-off.

3

Track it all in QuickBooks for your team to reconcile.

Every bill, payment, settlement, and credit writes into the client's QuickBooks Online, pre-coded to the accounts you map at setup. A daily Cleared sync ties it to the bank feed. Your bookkeeper opens the month to clean, matched entries instead of a POS export to rebuild.

The same engine works the other direction for clients who wholesale. When a brand is owed money, ShelfSpace invoices on ship, runs the reminders, and collects the overdue receivable through a Pay Now flow the buyer pays from their own bank — without handing a collection agency a 25% to 50% contingency cut of money that was always the client's. And on the AP side, it builds the monthly credit memos for returns, expired product, and co-marketing that a ledger records but never goes hunting for.

Built for cannabis, not adapted to it

General AP tools break on cannabis because the money doesn't move like other industries'. Card rails decline the transactions. Invoices have to reconcile against Metrc, not just a PO. Consignment settles on a weekly cycle against real sell-through. ShelfSpace is a certified Metrc third-party vendor, and the whole platform is built around those facts — which is why it catches the duplicate line a generalist tool waves through and why the settlement report both the retailer and the vendor trust actually ties out. Frequently asked questions

Does ShelfSpace compete with my accounting firm?

No. ShelfSpace does not do bookkeeping, month-end close, tax preparation, 280E, or fractional CFO work. It runs the payment operations underneath the ledger — verifying what a cannabis client owes against Metrc, moving payments on the operator's approval, and writing every transaction to QuickBooks. That's the work that clogs your engagement and that a general ledger was never built to do. You keep everything else, and your books arrive cleaner.

What exactly does ShelfSpace do for a cannabis client?

Three things. It verifies what's owed by three-way matching each vendor invoice against the Metrc manifest and the delivery, so duplicates and phantom lines get caught before a check is cut. It moves the payments once the operator approves them, using Check 21 checks vendors can deposit. And it recovers the vendor credits and collects the overdue receivables a client is owed. No. The retailer signs with ShelfSpace for the AP and AR work only. They stay your client for the close, reconciliations, payroll, financial statements, tax preparation, 280E, and advisory.
