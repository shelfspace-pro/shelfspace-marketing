What a cannabis debt collection agency actually costs you

Cannabis vendors get squeezed on payment terms harder than almost any other industry. Retailers stretch net-30 into net-60 and net-90, and because most cannabis debt can't move through normal banking channels, the money is genuinely hard to chase. So a whole category of cannabis debt collection agencies exists to chase it for you — CannaBIZ Collects is the best known.

Here's how that business model works. Agencies collect on contingency: they take a percentage of whatever they recover. CannaBIZ states its own terms plainly — "No Collection. No Fee. Period." — and confirms it charges "a contingency fee based on the total amount that was collected." What it doesn't publish is the number. Most agencies don't, because it varies with the age and difficulty of the debt, but across commercial and cannabis collections the typical range is 25% to 50% of what's recovered.

Run the math on a single invoice. A retailer owes you $10,000. At a 30% contingency — squarely mid-range — the agency keeps $3,000 and you net $7,000. Now scale it to a real ledger: say you're owed $80,000 across six slow-paying retailers. Recover all of it through an agency at 30% and you've handed over $24,000 — money you were already owed, for product you already delivered.

And that assumes full recovery, which contingency collections rarely deliver. Agencies pursue debt settlement — they negotiate a payoff for less than face value. So the more honest version of the example is: the agency settles your $10,000 invoice for $7,000, then takes its cut of that. You wanted ten grand and you delivered ten grand of product; you end up with a few thousand.

The deeper problem: you only call an agency after it's already bad

The contingency model has a built-in flaw for you: an agency only enters the picture after an invoice has already defaulted. Nobody hands a current invoice to a collections firm. By the time you do, weeks or months have passed, the retailer has learned that slow-paying you carries no consequence, and — if they're in real trouble — the cash may already be gone to whoever pushed hardest first.

Worse, handing a retailer to a collections agency ends the relationship. The moment a demand letter or an attorney shows up, you're no longer a partner they buy from next month — you're a creditor they're fighting. For a vendor who wants to keep selling into that store, that's a heavy price on top of the fee.

The thing nobody sells you, because there's no contingency fee in it, is prevention. The cheapest dollar to collect is the one you collect on time, from a retailer who still wants to do business with you.

ShelfSpace is cannabis collections software, not an agency. Instead of waiting for an invoice to die and then taking a cut to resurrect it, it works the entire time the invoice is open:

  • Automated dunning in your name. A steady, professional cadence that firms up as the invoice ages — 30, 60, 90 days — sent on schedule so you're never the one typing "just following up." It reads like your business, not a collections firm, so the relationship stays intact.

It runs across your whole ledger, continuously — not one debt at a time after the fact. That's the difference between a fire extinguisher and a fire department: the point is that the fire never gets big enough to need the trucks.

Where a collection agency still makes sense

Be honest about the one case where an agency is the right call: a debt that's genuinely dead. The retailer has gone dark or insolvent, you've already written the invoice off, and you're prepared to end the relationship and possibly take it to court. That's a legal fight, and it's what agencies and their attorney networks are built for. ShelfSpace isn't a litigator and won't pretend to be one.

But notice what that case actually is: the last resort, after prevention already failed and the relationship is already over. The reason so many cannabis invoices end up there is that nothing was working them while they were still collectable. Close that gap and the number of debts that ever reach an agency drops sharply — which is exactly the job ShelfSpace does.

An agency takes a quarter to half of a debt after it's already gone bad. Software that collects while the invoice is still current — for a flat fee, with you keeping 100% — means most debts never get there.
