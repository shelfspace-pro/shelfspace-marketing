Cannabis Collections Agency vs. Collecting in Your Own Name

A store owes you for three deliveries and stopped answering. An agency will take the account and a cut of whatever it recovers. Most of the time there's a faster way that keeps the money and the customer.

At a glance

  • A cannabis collections agency takes over an overdue account and keeps a percentage of what it recovers. The rate usually climbs as the debt gets older.
  • Agencies are usually called in late, after months of silence, and the store hears from a stranger instead of from your brand.
  • ShelfSpace is not a collections agency. It's software that keeps collections in your brand's name: pay links, a reminder schedule, AI calls, payment plans and final notice letters.
  • Most overdue dispensaries aren't refusing to pay. They need an easy way to pay and someone who keeps asking.
  • An agency or an attorney is still the right call when a store has closed, gone silent after every step, or refuses to pay a balance it owes.

If you're searching for a cannabis collections agency, you're probably past the point of patience. The invoices are 60, 90, 120 days old. Your sales rep has sent the "just checking in" text four times. The buyer who placed the order has left, and nobody at the store seems to know who pays the bills.

An agency feels like the grown-up move. Sometimes it is. But for most cannabis brands, handing the account to an agency is the most expensive way to collect, and it's usually done far later than it needs to be. This guide lays out what an agency does, what keeping collections in your own name looks like, and how to tell which one an account needs.

What a cannabis collections agency actually does

A collections agency takes over the debt. You send them the invoices, the store's contact details and proof the product was delivered. From then on, the agency contacts the store, not you. Most work on contingency: they keep a percentage of whatever they recover, and you get the rest. If they recover nothing, you usually pay nothing.

The rate depends on the size and the age of the balance. Fresh debt recovers more often and costs less to place. Debt older than about 180 days recovers less and carries a higher contingency rate (Resolve Pay). That's the catch for cannabis brands: by the time most send an account to an agency, it's already in the most expensive, least collectible bracket.

The problem is big. Delinquent payments in the U.S. cannabis industry passed $4 billion in 2024, according to LeafLink. Plenty of that money is owed to brands that never followed up in a steady way, not to brands whose stores refused to pay.

The hidden cost: the account leaves your name

The fee is the part you can see. The bigger cost is the store. Once an agency calls, the relationship changes. The buyer who would have reordered next month now sees your brand as the company that sent them to collections. In a state with a few hundred licensed dispensaries, that's not a small thing to give up.

Agencies also do nothing to stop the next late invoice. They work the balance you hand them. Meanwhile your new invoices go out the same way they always have, with no pay link and no follow-up, and the next account starts drifting toward 90 days too.

Why most dispensaries pay late

Most stores that owe you aren't refusing to pay. They're short on staff, the person who pays bills is not the person who ordered, your invoice is buried under forty others, and paying it means finding a checkbook or setting you up in their bank. When nobody asks again, it slides another month.

That's why the fix is usually not more pressure. It's less friction and a steady schedule. Make paying one click, ask on a schedule that gets firmer as the bill ages, and make it easy to say yes. For more on why net-30 drifts, see Cannabis Net Terms: How to Get Invoices Paid.

When an agency or an attorney is the right call

Not every balance comes back, and we'll tell you so. Some stores close, and some disputes are real. After every account has been worked, we tell you plainly which balances are coming back and which ones are a business decision for you. An agency or an attorney is worth it when:

  • The store has closed or changed hands. The license is surrendered, the doors are shut, or a new owner says the old debt isn't theirs. Recovering that money takes legal steps that software can't take.
  • The store has gone silent after every step. Reminders, phone calls, a payment plan offer and a final notice letter, and still nothing. Silence after all of that is a signal.
  • The store refuses to pay a balance it owes. If the store agrees it got the product but won't pay, and you have the delivery records, an attorney's demand letter or a lawsuit may be the only lever left.
  • The balance is very old and very large. Past 180 days, the odds drop quickly. A large balance at that age deserves professional help.

Your state may also give you a lever before court. New York's Office of Cannabis Management, for example, runs a delinquent payment reporting process for retailers that don't pay suppliers on credit terms. A final notice letter can list referral to an agency or an attorney, or a report to the state regulator, as consequences, but only the ones you'll really follow through on. This isn't legal advice. Check with your own attorney before you threaten or take legal action.

The order matters. Work the account in your own name first. You'll collect most balances without giving up a cut or a customer, and the few that are left go to an agency with a clean paper trail: every invoice, every reminder, every call and every promise, dated.

How to choose for each overdue account

Sort your open invoices by how late they are, then ask three questions about each store. Is it still open? Has anyone followed up on a schedule, with an easy way to pay? Is there a real dispute? If the store is open, nobody has followed up properly and there's no dispute, it belongs in your own collections, not with an agency. If it's closed, silent after every step, or refusing, that's when to bring in help.

Frequently asked questions

What is a cannabis collections agency?

A cannabis collections agency is a company that takes over a brand's overdue dispensary invoices and contacts the store to collect. Most work on contingency, keeping a percentage of whatever they recover, and the rate usually rises as the debt gets older.

Is ShelfSpace a collections agency?

No. ShelfSpace is collections software for cannabis brands, not a collections agency. It doesn't buy debt or take over accounts. Every invoice, reminder, call and letter goes out in the brand's own name, and stores pay the brand directly.

Should a cannabis brand use a collections agency or collect in its own name?

Collect in your own name first. Most overdue dispensaries pay once paying is easy and someone follows up on a schedule. Save an agency or an attorney for stores that have closed, gone silent after every step, or refuse to pay a balance they owe.

When should a cannabis brand send an account to collections?

When the store has closed or changed hands, has not responded to reminders, calls, a payment plan offer and a final notice letter, or refuses to pay a balance it agrees it owes. Very old, very large balances, past about 180 days, also justify professional help.
