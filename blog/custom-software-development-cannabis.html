Custom Software Development for Cannabis Companies: Build, Buy or Extend

Custom software makes sense for a cannabis company when the off-the-shelf tools can’t move money for you, can’t see across your licenses, or can’t talk to Metrc and your books. Here’s how to tell, what to build, and what to ask any developer before you sign.

Every cannabis company hits the same wall. The POS runs the store, Metrc runs compliance, QuickBooks or Xero runs the books, and the bank runs the money. Nothing connects them, so a person does: re-typing invoices, chasing receipts, building the same spreadsheet every Monday.

At some point someone says, “we should just build something.” Sometimes that’s right. Often it isn’t. This guide is for the owner, CFO or controller deciding which.

When off-the-shelf software is the right answer

Buy when a tool already does the job for cannabis and your process is close to everyone else’s. Point of sale, seed-to-sale tracking and standard accounting all have mature options. Building your own POS is almost never worth it.

A good test: if the vendor has cannabis customers who run the way you run, buy it and change your process to fit. Custom software you have to maintain forever is a real cost.

Four signs you need custom software

  • The tool won’t move money for you. Expense apps, bill pay and spend tools often capture the data fine and then stop at the payment, because their banking partners screen out cannabis. Expensify’s help center lists marijuana-related businesses among those it can’t process direct payments for, and BILL’s acceptable use policy bars plant-touching businesses.
  • You run several companies. Licenses usually mean separate legal entities with separate books. Most tools make you log in and out of each one. You want one screen, filtered by company, with each person seeing only the companies they work for.
  • Your systems don’t talk. If a person copies data from Metrc or your POS into your books every week, that’s an integration waiting to be built.
  • Your process is your edge. A consignment program, a credit policy or a rep commission plan that no product supports is worth building around, because it’s how you compete.

What makes cannabis software different

A developer who hasn’t built for cannabis will learn these on your project. Ask about each one up front.

  • Metrc. Many states track every cannabis package in Metrc. A tool that reads transfers, sales or inventory needs Metrc access set up the way the state requires, and has to keep working when Metrc changes its API.
  • Payments. Paying vendors, employees or yourself from a cannabis company’s account needs a bank and payment provider that accept the industry. Get that answered in writing before any code is written.
  • Separate books for each license. Every expense, bill and payment has to land in the right entity’s books, with the right account and tracking category.
  • 280E. Under section 280E, how you code costs affects your tax bill. Software should let your CPA set the accounts, not guess them.
  • Access control. Cannabis companies have owners, managers, budtenders, sales reps and outside bookkeepers. Every one should see only what their job needs, with a second factor on anything that moves money.

Build from scratch, or extend a platform

There are three ways to get custom software, and they differ mostly in how much plumbing you pay for.

  • Build from scratch with a general developer. You own every decision, and you pay for logins, permissions, hosting, the Metrc connection, the accounting sync and the payment rail before the first feature that’s actually yours.
  • Hire a freelancer to glue tools together. Cheap to start, but the person who understands the glue often leaves, and the tools it connects change without warning.
  • Extend a cannabis platform. Start from software that already handles payments, Metrc, Xero or QuickBooks, permissions and hosting, and build only what’s unique to you. You pay for less, and you get it sooner.

What to ask a developer before you sign

  • Have you connected to Metrc, and in which states?
  • How will this tool pay people, and which bank or payment provider has agreed to handle cannabis money?
  • How does it handle several companies with separate books?
  • Who hosts it, who fixes it at night, and who updates it when Metrc or our accounting software changes?
  • Can we use a working piece before the whole thing is done?

An example: expense management across several companies

A multi-state operator we work with runs several companies, each on its own Xero. Mainstream expense apps either wouldn’t pay their employees back or made them switch between companies all day. Here’s what we’re building with them:

  • Employees snap receipts from a phone and pick the company each expense belongs to.
  • One approver per employee, then a final check on coding by finance.
  • Approved reports post to the right company’s Xero as a bill, with the receipts attached.
  • Employees are paid back by bank transfer from the company’s own account, and the payment is recorded on the bill.
  • Every feature switches on per company and per person, so the AR team sees collections and not expenses.

The payment rail, the Xero connection and the permissions already existed. The project paid for the expense workflow itself. You can see the result on our expense reports page.

Frequently asked questions

What is custom software development for cannabis companies?

It’s software built around one cannabis company’s own process, usually to fill a gap that off-the-shelf tools leave: paying people and vendors, seeing several licenses on one screen, or connecting Metrc, the POS and the books so nobody re-types data.

Should a cannabis company build its own POS or seed-to-sale system?

Almost never. Point of sale and seed-to-sale tracking have mature cannabis products. Custom software pays off in the gaps between those systems and in the money side, where mainstream tools often won’t serve cannabis.

Why do cannabis companies need special payment software?

Many mainstream payment and spend tools restrict cannabis. Brex’s prohibited activities list names marijuana-related activity, and BILL’s acceptable use policy bars plant-touching businesses. A tool that pays employees or vendors needs a payment rail that accepts the industry.

Can custom software connect to Metrc?

Yes, through Metrc’s API, with access set up the way your state requires. Ask any developer which states they’ve connected in and who keeps the connection working when Metrc changes.
