Cannabis Cash Management Guide

How Dispensaries Handle Cash: Deposits, Transport, Payroll and Federal Taxes

Cash management in a dispensary touches the bank, the armored truck, the payroll run and the IRS. Here's how each one works, and which ones you can stop doing in cash.

At a glance

  • Dispensaries usually deposit through a bank or credit union that accepts cannabis funds, and banks report currency transactions over $10,000 as routine.
  • Never split deposits to dodge that report, because structuring is its own federal crime.
  • Federal tax deposits must be electronic, and the IRS says a 10 percent penalty applies otherwise, with case-by-case relief for a business without a bank account.
  • In-person cash payments at an IRS office go by appointment, booked 30 to 60 days ahead.
  • Vendor payments are the fastest cash to eliminate, since the other side is a licensed business that usually has a bank account.

Somebody at your store counts the drawers, seals the bag and worries about the drive to the bank. Then payroll, a vendor invoice and a federal tax deadline each ask for that same cash in a different form. If you're trying to work out how dispensaries actually handle all this, or how to do less of it, here's the practical version.

Why cannabis is a cash business

Cannabis is a cash business mainly because card networks won't carry the sale and banking access stays limited. A GAO report from August 2026 says Mastercard and Visa prohibit using their cards to buy cannabis, and that focus group participants reported high fees as a result, such as ATM-use, cash deposit and cash transport fees. The National Taxpayer Advocate has written that federal law limits access to financial institutions and that many of these businesses run on cash alone.

Having a bank account doesn't end the problem. In GAO's focus group poll, 43 of 48 participants said they had a business bank account, yet they still reported account closures and high fees. About 1,000 banks and credit unions filed cannabis-related suspicious activity reports in 2024, roughly 11 percent of insured institutions, and GAO cautions that the figure doesn't show how many take cannabis businesses as ongoing customers. Your shoppers may also keep paying in cash, as we cover in our guide to cannabis payment processing.

How dispensaries deposit money

Dispensaries deposit money through a bank or credit union that has agreed to take cannabis funds, either by walking it to a branch or by having an armored carrier pick it up. The institution expects your deposits to match your books. FinCEN's 2014 guidance, FIN-2014-G001, lists deposits larger than your reported cannabis revenue as a red flag it tells banks to watch for.

Large deposits generate paperwork, and that's routine. Under 31 CFR 1010.311, a financial institution files a report on any currency transaction of more than $10,000. Never split a deposit to stay under that line. Breaking up cash to evade a report is itself a crime under 31 U.S.C. 5324, with up to 5 years in prison for a basic violation, even when the money was earned legally, and FinCEN's guidance names apparently structured deposits as a red flag. Deposit what you took in, on a regular schedule, and let the report get filed.

  • Keep your numbers aligned. Your POS totals, cash log, deposit slips and tax filings should all tell the same story.
  • Keep your documents ready. FinCEN's guidance has banks verify your state license and watch your activity, so have those records on hand.
  • Know your fallback. A cannabis program can end with little notice. GAO reports account closures among the common complaints, so keep a second relationship in mind. Our bank directory and cannabis banking guide are good places to start.

Cannabis cash logistics and armored transport

Cannabis cash logistics means counting, storing and moving cash under controls, usually with an armored carrier taking it from your store to a processing facility for deposit. One credit union that serves the industry, Salal, in an article dated 05/05/2026, says many providers offer provisional deposit credit on the business day after a pickup, with the cash counted a second time before final deposit.

When you interview a carrier, ask where your cash goes and who holds it. FinCEN's ruling FIN-2014-R010 exempts a currency transporter from money transmitter rules only if, among other conditions, it holds no more than a custodial interest and moves the cash from one person to that same person at another location or to that person's account at a financial institution. Delivering cash to someone else takes it outside the exemption. So having a carrier hand your cash to a vendor is a different arrangement from a deposit run, and you should ask how the carrier is registered before you try it.

Inside the store, the controls are simple and worth writing down:

  • Two people count, and both sign the count.
  • Numbered sealed bags, with a log of who held each bag and when.
  • Deposit runs on varied routes and times if you carry it yourself, and never one employee alone.
  • Cash limits at the drawer, with drops to the safe through the day.
  • A check of your state's security rules and your insurance policy's cash limits, since both vary.

Theft is the reason for all of it. Representatives from four associations told GAO that holding large amounts of cash makes these businesses targets for theft and robbery.

How dispensaries pay their employees

Dispensaries pay employees by direct deposit or check through a payroll provider when they can, and in cash when providers drop them. GAO reports that in seven focus groups at least one participant said a payroll provider had closed or suspended service, and that participants in three focus groups turned to manual methods such as paying in cash.

Cash wages are allowed federally, and they don't reduce your paperwork. The federal rule at 29 CFR 531.27 requires wages in cash or a negotiable instrument payable at par, so cash is permitted, though your state may add pay stub and timing rules. The IRS says in Publication 15 that wages subject to employment taxes can be paid in cash or other forms, and that you must withhold federal income tax from each payment. Cash payroll therefore still means withholding, W-2s and deposits, plus a signed receipt for every envelope. Working in cannabis already complicates personal finances: GAO found that about half of 44 polled owners and managers said they or their employees had faced personal banking and loan challenges because of it. A pay history with no bank trail won't make that easier.

How dispensaries pay federal taxes

Dispensaries pay federal taxes electronically wherever they can, because federal tax deposits have to be electronic, and in-person cash payments go by appointment at an IRS office that accepts them. The IRS says businesses cannot use cash or card payments to make federal tax deposits. Deposits go through a business tax account, Direct Pay for businesses or EFTPS, and Publication 15 says all federal tax deposits must use electronic funds transfer. Ask your CPA which of your payments count as deposits.

That rule has a price. The IRS cash payment FAQ says a business that doesn't deposit electronically faces a 10 percent penalty, and that relief may be available case by case for a business without a bank account, if it asks. Late deposits also carry their own IRS penalty tiers of 2, 5, 10 and 15 percent, depending on how late they are. Ask your CPA whether to request relief, and don't assume you'll get it.

If you go to an IRS office with cash, call 844-545-5640 first, since not every office accepts it. Bring the exact amount bundled in the highest denominations you can, a valid, unexpired government-issued ID and your EIN. The IRS says it can take up to 2 hours, and to allow up to 2 weeks for the payment to show on your account. The same FAQ says last-minute large cash appointments usually can't be accommodated, so book early and keep your quarterly dates on a calendar.

State taxes follow their own rules. In California, the tax agency has a no-cash policy, may grant an exemption for undue hardship, and, if it approves you, requires a call at least 21 days ahead for an appointment. Check your own state's agency, and see our 280E guide and 280E calculator to size the bill you're planning around.

Cash you receive from another business has its own IRS form

Any business that receives more than $10,000 in cash in one transaction or in related transactions generally files Form 8300 within 15 days, under the IRS Form 8300 reference guide. That covers a vendor taking cash from a store. The guide also says personal checks and wire transfers don't count as cash, which is one more reason to move wholesale payments onto a bank rail.

The aggregation trips people up: the guide says payments from one payer within 24 hours are combined, and so are connected payments made further apart. Installments count once the total received within one year of the first payment passes the threshold. A written statement to each person named on the form is due by 01/31 of the following year. Your accountant should own this calendar.

How to get cash out of your workflows

Get cash out by moving your largest and most repetitive payments first, because you can change who you pay electronically long before you can change how shoppers pay you. Work through it in this order:

  • Vendor payments. Wholesale invoices are your biggest and easiest win, since the other side is a licensed business that usually has a bank account. No more cash runs to a distributor, and the record writes itself.
  • Everyone else you pay. Rent, service providers and contractors can often take a check or ACH from the same screen.
  • Payroll. Find a provider that knows your business is cannabis, in writing, so you aren't dropped when they find out.
  • Federal tax deposits. Pay electronically from your business account and stop losing a percentage to the penalty.
  • The register. Add a pay-by-bank option, and each shopper who uses it is cash you don't count, store or haul.
  • Your books. Tie every payment to QuickBooks or Xero as it happens. See our guide to cannabis bookkeeping for the routine.

Frequently asked questions

How do dispensaries deposit money?

They deposit through a bank or credit union that has agreed to take cannabis funds, by walking it in or using an armored carrier. Large currency deposits trigger a routine report from the institution. Deposit what you took in on a regular schedule, and never split deposits to avoid that report, since that is a separate crime.

How do dispensaries pay their employees?

They use direct deposit or checks through a payroll provider. Some have had providers drop them and turned to cash. Cash wages are allowed federally, but you still owe withholding, W-2s and electronic tax deposits, and your state may add its own rules.

How do dispensaries pay federal taxes?

Federal tax deposits have to be made electronically, from a bank account. In-person cash payments go by appointment at select IRS offices, and you should book well ahead of the due date. A business that doesn't deposit electronically may face a penalty, and one without a bank account can ask the IRS for case-by-case relief.

Why is cannabis a cash business?

Card networks prohibit cannabis purchases on their cards, and many banks and credit unions still won't serve the industry or serve it only at a high cost. That leaves shoppers and businesses leaning on cash. Pay-by-bank at the register and ACH between businesses are alternatives to it.

Can a dispensary pay vendors without cash?

Yes, if it has a bank account that accepts cannabis funds. ACH, mailed checks and e-checks all work between licensed businesses, and they leave a cleaner record than a cash run. We send all three through our banking partners from one screen.
