Past Due Invoice Email Templates for Cannabis Brands

Eight reminders you can copy and send today, from the friendly heads-up before the due date to the firm note at 30 days late. Plus a payment plan offer and a text your rep can send a buyer.

At a glance

  • Eight copy-paste templates: before the due date, due today, 7, 14 and 30 days past due, a payment plan offer, a promise-to-pay confirmation and a rep text.
  • Every one asks for one thing (payment or a pay date) and carries one link to pay.
  • Every one gives the buyer an easy out: already paid, or something on the invoice is wrong.
  • They get firmer as the invoice ages. Only list a consequence you'll really follow through on.
  • Send them in your own name, from your accounts receivable team. Past that, a final notice letter is the next step.

Most past-due wholesale invoices in cannabis aren't a fight. They're a buyer with forty emails, an owner who approves payments on Thursdays, and a question about one line on the invoice that nobody answered. A clear past due invoice email, sent on time, fixes most of them.

The trouble is writing it. "Just following up" for the fifth time feels awkward, so it doesn't get sent, and the invoice ages another month. Below are the templates we'd use: copy them, fill in the brackets, and send. Each one has a note on when to send it and who it should come from.

The reminder schedule

Space the emails out and let the tone firm up as the invoice gets older. Here's the order the templates follow:

1

A few days before the due date

A friendly heads-up with the link to pay. Catches invoice questions while there's still time.

2

The due date

Two lines. It's due today, here's the link.

3

7 and 14 days late

Ask what's holding it up, then ask for a pay date. Offer a plan if paying all at once is the problem.

4

30 days late

Name your terms, set a pay-by date, and say what happens next.

5

Still nothing

A one-page final notice letter on your letterhead, with a firm date.

Fill in every bracket before you send. A reminder with the wrong amount or an old due date gives the buyer a reason to set it aside.

The templates

1. Friendly reminder before the due date

Before it's due

Subject: Invoice [#] for [amount] is due [due date]Hi [first name],

Quick heads-up from [Brand]: invoice [#] for [amount] is due on [due date]. You can pay it here in a minute, no login needed: [pay link]

If anything on the invoice looks off, reply and tell me which line, and we'll sort it out before the due date.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Brand] Accounts Receivable

When: 3 to 5 days before the due date. From: your AR inbox. This is the one that catches a wrong price or a short delivery while there's still time to fix it.

2. Due today

Due today

Subject: Invoice [#] is due todayHi [first name],

Invoice [#] for [amount] is due today. Here's the link to pay: [pay link]

Already sent it? Thank you. Reply with the payment date and we'll match it up.

[Your name]
[Brand] Accounts Receivable

When: the morning of the due date. From: your AR inbox. Keep it short. The "already sent it?" line saves you from chasing money that's on its way.

3. 7 days past due

7 days late

Subject: Invoice [#] is 7 days past dueHi [first name],

Invoice [#] for [amount] was due on [due date] and is now 7 days past due. Could you get it paid this week? [pay link]

If it's stuck on your side (waiting on approval, a question about the order, or you need a copy of the invoice), tell me what you need and I'll send it today.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Brand] Accounts Receivable

When: one week after the due date. From: your AR inbox. Assume it's a hold-up, not a refusal, and offer to clear it.

4. 14 days past due

14 days late

Subject: Second notice: invoice [#], 14 days past dueHi [first name],

I haven't seen payment for invoice [#] ([amount], due [due date]). It's now 14 days past due.

Can you reply with the date it will be paid? If paying it all at once is hard right now, tell me and we can split it into a few payments.

Pay here: [pay link]

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Brand] Accounts Receivable

When: two weeks after the due date. From: your AR inbox. Asking for a date gets an answer more often than asking for money. Write the date down when you get it.

5. 30 days past due (firm)

30 days late

Subject: Invoice [#] is 30 days past due: payment needed by [pay-by date]Hi [first name],

Invoice [#] for [amount] was due on [due date] under our [Net 30] terms. It's now 30 days past due.

Please pay by [pay-by date]: [pay link]

If we don't have payment or a firm pay date by then, we'll need to [move your account to payment on delivery / hold new orders] until the balance is current.

I'd much rather keep shipping to you on terms. Reply with a date and I'll hold off.

[Your name]
[Brand] Accounts Receivable

When: 30 days after the due date. From: your AR team or an owner, never the rep. Set the pay-by date about a week out. Only name a consequence you'll really follow through on. If your state sets its own deadline for paying on credit, you can mention it here.

6. Payment plan offer

Payment plan

Subject: Need more time on invoice [#]? Here's an optionHi [first name],

If paying invoice [#] ([amount]) in one go is tough right now, we can split it into [number] [weekly / monthly] payments of about [payment amount], with no interest.

Reply "yes" and I'll send the schedule. Or pay in full any time here: [pay link]

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Brand] Accounts Receivable

When: any time after 14 days late, or the moment a buyer says cash is tight. From: your AR inbox. A plan the store can keep beats a full payment it can't make. More on how plans work: Cannabis Payment Plans: Getting Paid by Dispensaries.

7. Promise-to-pay confirmation

Confirm a promise

Subject: Confirming: invoice [#] paid by [promised date]Hi [first name],

Thanks for getting back to me. Just confirming what we agreed: you'll pay invoice [#] ([amount]) by [promised date]. I'll hold off on reminders until then.

Here's the link for when you're ready: [pay link]

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Brand] Accounts Receivable

When: the same day a buyer gives you a date, by phone, text or email. From: whoever got the promise. Then check on the date. A promise nobody checks on is how an invoice slips another month.

8. A text your sales rep can send

Rep text

Hi [first name], [rep name] from [Brand]. Quick note: invoice [#] for [amount] was due [due date]. You can pay it here in a minute: [pay link]. If there's an issue with the order, just text me back.

When: anywhere from 7 to 30 days late, when emails aren't getting answered. From: the rep who knows the buyer. Keep it friendly and leave the firm messages to your AR team, so the rep stays the person the buyer wants to hear from. More: Cannabis Sales Rep Collections, Every Monday.

When the emails stop working

If you've sent the 30-day email and heard nothing, another reminder isn't a plan. The next step is one page on your own letterhead: what's owed, a firm pay-by date, a way to pay, and only the consequences you'll really follow through on. We wrote that one up separately, with what to put on it: A Final Notice Letter for Overdue Cannabis Invoices.

Five rules that get past due invoices paid

  1. Send on schedule. The reminder that gets skipped because it feels awkward is the one that lets the invoice age into a problem.
  2. One ask, one link. Ask for payment or a pay date, and put the way to pay right in the email. Every extra step is a reason to put it off.
  3. Always give an out. "Already paid?" and "Something wrong on the invoice?" turn a silent buyer into a reply.
  4. Send it in your name. The store has a relationship with your brand. Keep the reminders coming from you, not a stranger.
  5. Mean every consequence. A threat you don't carry out teaches the store to ignore the next email.

Frequently asked questions

When should a cannabis brand send a past due invoice reminder?

Start before the invoice is late. Send a friendly heads-up a few days before the due date, a short note on the due date, then reminders at 7, 14 and 30 days past due. Each one should get a little firmer, and every one should carry a link the store can pay from.

What should a past due invoice email say?

The invoice number, the amount, the due date, how many days late it is, and one way to pay. Ask for one thing: payment or a pay date. Give the buyer an easy way to say it was already paid or that something on the invoice is wrong, because a question nobody answered is a common reason a wholesale cannabis invoice sits unpaid.

Should a sales rep send payment reminders to a dispensary?

A short, friendly text from the rep who knows the buyer often works when emails don't. Keep the firm messages coming from the brand's accounts receivable team, so the rep stays the person the buyer wants to hear from.

How firm should a 30 days past due email be?

Firm and specific: name your payment terms, give a pay-by date about a week out, and say what happens if it isn't paid, such as moving the account to payment on delivery. Only list a step you will really take. If your state sets its own deadline for paying on credit, you can mention it.

When should a brand send a final notice letter instead of another email?

When several reminders have gone unanswered and the invoice is well past due, often around 60 days. A one-page letter on the brand's own letterhead, with a pay-by date and a way to pay, gets read when another reminder won't.
