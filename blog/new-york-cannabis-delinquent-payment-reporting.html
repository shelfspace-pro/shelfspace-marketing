New York Cannabis Delinquent Payment Rules: 30 Days, a Notice of Default, and the C.O.D. List

If you sell to New York dispensaries on credit, the state sets the clock. Here is what the Office of Cannabis Management's own guidance says happens on day 30, what you have to send and report, and how a store gets back off the list.

At a glance

  • A New York dispensary that buys cannabis on credit must pay in full within 30 days of receiving the product in Metrc, unless the Cannabis Control Board approves otherwise.
  • If it doesn't, the supplier must send a written notice of default and report the delinquency to the Office no later than 7 calendar days after the final payment date.
  • Reported stores go on the C.O.D. list. Suppliers can't sell to them on credit, only for cash at delivery.
  • Since 09/15/2026, delinquencies are reported in your Metrc account. Report payment in full within 1 business day of receiving it.
  • Reporting is not optional, and the Office does not collect the money for you. Getting paid before day 30 is still on you.

A store in Brooklyn took a delivery on credit. Day 30 came and went. Now a state rule decides what you do next, on a schedule you don't set, and skipping a step can put your own license at risk.

New York is one of the few cannabis markets with a written, enforced rule for New York cannabis delinquent payment reporting. It lives in Section 124.2 of Title 9 of the New York Codes, Rules and Regulations (9 NYCRR § 124.2), and the Office of Cannabis Management (OCM) explains it in its Delinquent Payment Reporting Guidance (Rev. 10/19/2026) and its Delinquent Payments FAQs (Rev. 09/16/2026). Everything below comes from those two documents and the OCM Delinquent Payment Reporting page.

This is a plain-English summary, not legal advice. The rules can change. Confirm anything that affects your license with OCM at CODReporting@ocm.ny.gov or with your own attorney.

Who the rule covers

The guidance applies to licensed suppliers and retailers. It defines a supplier as a licensed distributor, cooperative, microbusiness, Registered Organization with Dispensing (ROD) or Registered Organization Non-Dispensing (ROND) authorized to distribute cannabis products to licensed retailers. The retailer is the dispensary buying from them.

Selling on credit is a choice. In the FAQ's words, suppliers "MAY, but are not required to" sell on credit. If you do, the rules below apply to every credit sale. If a separate distributor sells your product to the store, confirm with them which of you holds the reporting duty for those sales.

The 30-day rule

A retailer buying on credit must pay for the product in full within 30 days, unless the Cannabis Control Board approves otherwise. The guidance is specific about when the clock starts. The payment period begins on the date the products are electronically received by the retailer and ends on the thirtieth day after that. The last day is the final payment date.

That detail matters more than it looks. Your invoice may say Net 30 from the invoice date, but the state counts from the day the store accepts the transfer in Metrc. If those two dates differ, the state's date is the one that decides whether a store is delinquent. OCM publishes the exact dates each year in its 2026 Credit Calendar.

You may see a 90-day figure quoted in older articles. The current OCM guidance and FAQs say 30 days, unless the Cannabis Control Board approves otherwise, and neither document mentions a 90-day period.

The written notice of default

Before a store lands on the list, it must hear from you. The guidance says the supplier must send a written notice of default to a retailer that missed its final payment date, and sets three conditions:

  • How it's sent: by first class mail, unless you and the retailer agreed on another written form of notice.
  • When: on or before the notification dates the Office sets on its Credit Calendar. The FAQ puts it as no later than 7 calendar days after the final payment date.
  • What you keep: a copy of the notice, for at least five years from the date you created it, readily retrievable if the Office asks (9 NYCRR § 125.13).

The OCM's own FAQ for retailers says "No retailer should be surprised that they have been added to the C.O.D. list." The notice is why.

How to report a dispensary for non-payment in New York

Reporting moved to Metrc. The guidance says that effective 09/15/2026, any new report of a delinquent payment must be completed in Metrc, following Metrc Bulletin NY_IB_0025, "Delinquency Tracking – Cash on Delivery (COD)." The FAQ adds that the old C.O.D. Reporting Platform took reports for deliveries made before 09/15/2026 only through 10/22/2026, and its delinquency reporting was turned off on 10/23/2026.

Two rules people miss:

  • It's mandatory. The FAQ says reporting "is not optional; it is required pursuant to 9 NYCRR §124.2 (d)(1)." A supplier who fails to report may face disciplinary action, and the Board may revoke, cancel or suspend a license for failing to comply with § 124.2.
  • Report payment, too. When a reported store pays in full, you must tell the Office within 1 business day. Report it where you reported the delinquency: in Metrc for Metrc reports, on the legacy platform for legacy reports, per OCM's "How to Report Payment in Full" instructions. Do not report a partial payment.

Counts as payment in full

The full invoice amount due for that delivery date, before any interest or late fees. Report it within 1 business day, even if the store hasn't paid your late fees.

Doesn't count

A partial payment. The FAQ says plainly: do not report partial payment. The store stays delinquent on that delivery until the invoice amount is paid.

Life on the C.O.D. list

A store reported as having an unpaid balance older than 30 days goes on the C.O.D. (cash on delivery) list. Suppliers with the right Transfer Menu permissions can see the list in Metrc under Transfers, COD List, Retailers on COD. When you create a transfer to a store on the list, Metrc won't offer credit terms. You can still sell to that store, for cash at delivery. The guidance defines cash broadly: currency, certified check, money order, electronic funds transfer, a bank officer's check or draft, or a check drawn on the licensee's account.

Getting off the list takes payment in full to every supplier that reported the store, and each of those suppliers reporting it. The store comes off at the next publication date. Until then, the Office may approve credit sales in writing if three conditions are met: the owed supplier has reported the payment in full, the supplier who wants to extend credit asks the Office in writing, and the store has no other outstanding credits with other suppliers.

Disputes

If a store thinks it was reported in error, it should first work it out with you. If you can't resolve a dispute over whether it paid, how much is due or how much product was delivered, the store can file a dispute through the C.O.D. Standing view in its Metrc account and upload documents. Both sides send statements to CODReporting@ocm.ny.gov, the Office decides, and the store can appeal and ask for a hearing under 9 NYCRR Part 133. Your records are your defense, which is why the guidance asks you to keep invoices, receipts, purchase orders, delivery receipts and notices for five years.

The Office won't collect for you

This is the part that surprises brands. OCM's page says "The Office is not a collections agency or payment enforcer," and FAQ 16 says licensees are responsible for pursuing payment of their outstanding invoices. The C.O.D. list protects the next supplier. It doesn't put money in your account. The best outcome is a store that pays before day 30, so you never need the list at all.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a New York dispensary have to pay for cannabis bought on credit?

30 days. Under 9 NYCRR § 124.2, a retailer buying cannabis products on credit must pay in full within 30 days of receiving the product, unless the Cannabis Control Board approves otherwise. OCM's guidance starts the clock on the date the retailer electronically receives the products in Metrc and ends it on the thirtieth day after, which is the final payment date.

What happens if a New York dispensary doesn't pay within 30 days?

The supplier must send the retailer a written notice of default and report the delinquency to the Office of Cannabis Management no later than 7 calendar days after the final payment date. The retailer is then placed on the C.O.D. list, and no supplier may sell to it on credit, only for cash at the time of delivery, until it is removed.

How do I report a dispensary for non-payment in New York?

In your Metrc account. OCM's guidance says that effective 09/15/2026, any new report of a delinquent payment must be completed in Metrc, following Metrc Bulletin NY_IB_0025, Delinquency Tracking – Cash on Delivery (COD). Questions go to CODReporting@ocm.ny.gov.

Is reporting a delinquent dispensary optional in New York?

No. OCM's FAQ says reporting delinquent payments is not optional and is required under 9 NYCRR § 124.2(d)(1). Suppliers who fail to report may face disciplinary action, and the Cannabis Control Board may revoke, cancel or suspend a license for failing to comply with § 124.2.

Does the written notice of default have to be mailed?

OCM's guidance says the supplier must send the written notice of default by first class mail unless the retailer and supplier agreed on another written form of notification. It must go out on or before the notification date on OCM's Credit Calendar, and the supplier must keep a copy for at least five years.

How does a dispensary get off the New York C.O.D. list?

It must pay in full for the cannabis products it received on credit from every supplier that reported it, and each of those suppliers must report the payment in full within 1 business day. The retailer is removed at the next publication of the list. Before then, the Office may approve credit sales in writing if the payment was reported, the supplier requests it in writing, and the retailer has no other outstanding credits with other suppliers.

Can a New York cannabis supplier charge late fees or interest?

Yes. OCM's FAQ says suppliers may charge late fees or interest, but a retailer that pays the full invoice amount due for a delivery date must still be reported as paid in full within 1 business day, even if it hasn't paid the late fees or interest. Partial payments are not reported.

Is it 30 days or 90 days to pay in New York?

OCM's current Delinquent Payment Reporting Guidance (Rev. 10/19/2026) and Delinquent Payments FAQs (Rev. 09/16/2026) both say 30 days, unless the Cannabis Control Board approves otherwise. Neither document mentions a 90-day payment period.

Does the Office of Cannabis Management collect unpaid invoices for suppliers?

No. OCM says it is not a collections agency or payment enforcer, and its FAQ says licensees are responsible for pursuing payment of their outstanding invoices. The C.O.D. list stops other suppliers from extending credit; collecting the money is still up to the supplier.
