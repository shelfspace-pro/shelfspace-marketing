Expense Management for Cannabis Companies: Receipts, Approvals and Paying People Back

Expense management for a cannabis company comes down to three jobs: get the receipt in, get the right person to approve it, and pay the employee back. The first two are easy to buy. The third is where mainstream tools turn cannabis away.

A budtender buys gloves on the way in. A sales rep drives to three stores. A manager pays for a team lunch on a personal card. Every one of those is money the company owes someone, and every one needs a receipt, a code and an approval before anyone gets paid back.

This guide is for the controller or owner who's tired of shoeboxes and spreadsheets. It covers why the usual apps don't fit cannabis, what a multi-license operator actually needs, and how to set up a process your CPA will thank you for.

Why mainstream expense apps fit cannabis poorly

Most expense apps capture receipts well. The trouble starts when money has to move, because the payment side runs on banking partners that screen out cannabis. Here's what each provider's own pages say, as of 11/05/2026.

  • Expensify. Its help center lists marijuana-related businesses among the industries it "cannot process direct payments for", which covers ACH reimbursements and the Expensify Card.
  • BILL. Its acceptable use policy says plant-touching marijuana-related businesses, such as growers, processors and dispensaries, "may not use any BILL software or payment products and services". That includes the spend tool that used to be Divvy.
  • Brex. Its prohibited activities list names marijuana-related activity among the activities it prohibits.
  • Ramp. Its help center lists cannabis-related businesses as unable to use international reimbursements, and its Canadian application page says cannabis businesses may be restricted from onboarding. Ask Ramp directly about your US entity.

Xero's own tool is changing too. Xero's developer changelog says it's retiring Classic Expenses and disabling the old expense claims connection, with the receipts connection deprecated in February 2027. A side-by-side of the options is in our Expensify alternatives for cannabis guide.

What a cannabis company needs from expense management

A cannabis operator usually runs several licensed entities, often with different owners on each. That changes what "good" looks like.

  • An entity on every expense. A lunch for the Michigan team belongs to the Michigan entity, not whichever company the manager happens to work for. Pick the entity when the receipt comes in, and split reports by entity at submit.
  • Location and department tracking. Code each line to an account and to the tracking categories your books already use, such as store or department, so reports by location come out right.
  • Approvals with teeth. A manager approves, a second person approves anything over a set amount, then finance signs off. Nobody approves their own report.
  • Duplicate checks across people. The same receipt submitted by two employees, or to two entities, should get caught before anyone is paid twice.
  • A way to pay people. Reimbursement by bank transfer or check, or a clean file for payroll, from a provider that will actually work with your business.

Keep reimbursements under an accountable plan

An accountable plan is what keeps reimbursements off the employee's taxable wages. The IRS Publication 463 says that if the plan's rules are met, the employer shouldn't include the reimbursements in box 1 of the employee's W-2. The same publication sets a safe harbor for a reasonable period of time.

  • The employee accounts for the expense within 60 days after it was paid or incurred.
  • The employee returns any excess reimbursement within 120 days after the expense was paid or incurred.
  • Documentary evidence is needed for lodging, and for any other expense of $75 or more, per Publication 463.

An expense tool should flag a receipt that's older than your limit or a missing receipt over your threshold. Flagging rather than blocking is usually right, because the approver knows when an exception is fair.

Mileage changed mid-year in 2026

If you reimburse mileage at the IRS standard rate, one flat rate for 2026 is wrong. The IRS set the 2026 business rate at 72.5 cents per mile, and the Journal of Accountancy reports it rose to 76 cents per mile effective 07/01/2026. Price each trip at the rate for the date it was driven.

Coding expenses with 280E in mind

Under section 280E, a business trafficking in Schedule I cannabis can't deduct its operating expenses, and only cost of goods sold still reduces its income. That makes the account you pick for each expense matter more than it does for most businesses. Don't let the expense tool decide it for you. Agree a short list of accounts with your CPA, map each expense category to one of them, and keep the receipt attached to every line so the coding can be checked later. Frequently asked questions

What is expense management software?

It's the tool employees use to turn receipts into expense reports, managers use to approve them, and finance uses to code them, post them to the books and pay people back. For a cannabis company the last step is the hard one, because several mainstream tools won't move money for a marijuana-related business.

Can a cannabis company use Expensify, Ramp, Brex or BILL?

Check each provider's current policy before you sign up. Expensify's help center lists marijuana-related businesses among the industries it can't process direct payments for, BILL's acceptable use policy bars plant-touching businesses from its software and payments, and Brex's prohibited activities list names marijuana-related activity. Ramp's help center restricts cannabis from some features, such as international reimbursements.

How long does an employee have to turn in receipts?

Under the IRS safe harbor for accountable plans, employees account for expenses within 60 days after they were paid or incurred and return any excess reimbursement within 120 days. Receipts are needed for all lodging and for other expenses at or above the threshold in IRS Publication 463. Your CPA can tell you how your own plan should be written.

How do you track expenses across several cannabis licenses?

Give every expense an entity and a location when it's captured, not at month end. Reports then split by entity, each entity's books get only its own costs, and your CPA can see which spending belongs to which license.

Does ShelfSpace post expenses to QuickBooks?

Yes. Approved expense reports post to QuickBooks Online or Xero as a bill to the employee, with the receipts attached.
