How Consignment Works on ShelfSpace

Docs / Consignment
Consignment

At a Glance

  • Cannabis consignment platform — vendors place product, you pay after it sells
  • The vendor owns the inventory until a customer buys it
  • Settlements calculated from your POS data on your schedule — weekly by default, or biweekly/monthly per vendor — with category-level profit splits
  • The platform runs the settlement math, generates the check, and ships the vendor portal. You own the vendor relationship.

The Cannabis Consignment Platform Model

ShelfSpace is a cannabis-specific system for running consignment with your vendors. The platform tracks every SKU of vendor-owned inventory on your shelves through the weekly settlement cycle. Instead of paying for product upfront, your vendors deliver goods on consignment — they retain ownership until a sale happens at the register. You never tie up capital in inventory you haven't sold yet.

This model works especially well in cannabis, where product variety is high and shelf life is limited. Consignment lets you carry more SKUs, test new brands, and avoid getting stuck with slow movers — all without upfront cost. Learn more on our consignment service page.

What the platform runs: POS data ingestion, settlement calculations, aging discount application, profit split math, check generation, vendor portal access, and QuickBooks sync. You review each settlement summary, approve the payment, and own the vendor conversation.

Who Owns the Inventory

Under a consignment arrangement, the vendor retains legal ownership of the product until it sells. Your dispensary provides the shelf space, the staff, and the customer relationship. When a unit sells, the revenue is split between you and the vendor according to your agreed category splits.

Unsold inventory remains the vendor's responsibility. If product ages past a threshold, aging discounts kick in to keep shelves fresh and pricing competitive.

The Settlement Cycle

On your settlement schedule (weekly by default; biweekly or monthly if you set it per vendor), the platform pulls your POS sales data and runs the settlement calculation for each vendor. Here's the formula at a high level:

  • Gross sales — total revenue from that vendor's consigned products
  • Category splits — each product category has a negotiated vendor/retailer percentage
  • Aging discounts — deductions for product that sat on the shelf past agreed thresholds
  • Return credits — any credits for returned or defective product
  • Net payout — the vendor's check amount after all deductions

All math uses integer-precision arithmetic rounded to the penny, so there are no floating-point surprises. See weekly settlements for the full calculation breakdown.

What You Get Each Cycle

Every settlement produces a detailed settlement report — covering the settlement summary, sales detail, margin analysis, waste & credits, and remaining inventory. Vendors receive the same report through their portal, so both sides are looking at the same numbers.

The check is generated as a Check 21 payment and delivered digitally. Vendors can download it directly from the vendor portal.
